Use `ak:motion-video` to make a 1920×1080, 30 fps motion-graphic video where
scene cuts land on the music's beats, reveals land on spoken words, and the
audio mix is measured rather than guessed. The workflow is scripted end to end:
facts, script, generated audio, forced alignment, beat grid, bar-spliced music,
timeline and mix, HTML composition, lint and check, render, remux, social
encode, and optional 1:1 or 9:16 editions. It renders through HyperFrames (HTML
+ GSAP) and does not publish the result.

## Choose the motion-video workflow

### Use ak:motion-video when

- You need a launch, release, changelog, feature, or explainer video with
  voice-over, captions, SFX, and music synced to the beat.
- You want a restyle of an existing video as a separate project, or a partial
  rework such as new music, re-timed drops, a re-mix, a re-render, a social
  encode, or a square/vertical edition of one.
- You want one named style profile, or one base style with individual
  dimensions borrowed from other styles for particular scenes.
- Every on-screen fact can be traced to a primary source such as release notes,
  repository docs, or code.

### Choose another workflow when

- You need generative text-to-video footage. Use [`ak:video`](./video).
- The composition is a React or Remotion project. Use
  [`ak:remotion`](./remotion).
- You have a general HyperFrames question or want a plain HTML composition
  without the beat-sync pipeline. Use [`ak:hyperframes`](./hyperframes).
- You need talking-head editing. This Skill does not cover it.
- You want a router across every installed motion or video Skill. Use
  [`ak:motion-graphics`](./motion-graphics).

## Meet the production bar

The workflow targets a real production piece, not a demo: every scene needs a
focal point, depth, overlapping choreography, anticipation and follow-through,
micro-motion through its holds, and a transition that carries into the next
scene. Every scene passes a director's pass before the final render.

By default, a director subagent writes the creative direction and reviews the
draft for taste, and an executor subagent runs the pipeline and builds the
composition from that brief; the main session pins the outcome, owns the
brief, and verifies the result. Pass `--poor` for a single session with no
subagents, lean reads, a compact brief, and one review round when token spend
matters more than the full director/executor split — it never skips a
verification check.

## Prepare the production route

Pin the outcome before any file is touched: topic and sources, style, duration,
voice language, ending, and signature. English voice-over with Vietnamese
captions is the default. The Skill asks only when the style or the facts source
is unknown; when only the style is open, it runs a
[style bake-off](#compare-styles-with-a-bake-off) instead of asking in the
abstract.

The full route also needs:

- The `multix` CLI on `PATH`, configured with its own credentials for Gemini
  TTS voice, ElevenLabs SFX, ElevenLabs Music composition plans, and ElevenLabs
  forced alignment. Credentials stay in multix's config; the Skill never prints
  or copies them into project files, plans, or reports.
- Node.js to run the project scripts and the pinned
  `npx --yes hyperframes@0.8.77` commands.
- `ffmpeg` and `ffprobe` for the audio mix, remux, loudness and black-frame
  checks, and social encode.
- Python with NumPy and SciPy for the beat-grid and audio checks, and PyYAML
  for `scripts/resolve-style.py`.
- A writable project and enough storage for generated WAV and MP3 audio,
  snapshots, and 1080p MP4 renders.
- Only for [generated still art](#add-generated-still-art): multix image
  generation. With a signed-in `codex` CLI on `PATH`, the Skill uses multix's
  OpenAI image route through the local Codex CLI; otherwise it uses the Gemini
  image route. Both use multix's own configured credentials; the Skill adds no
  new API key.

| Runtime | Invocation | Availability boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Claude Code | `/ak:motion-video ...` | Native delivery is the default; explicit plugin delivery is also supported. Full production still depends on multix, provider access, Node, Python, and FFmpeg in the session environment. |
| Cursor | `/ak:motion-video ...` | Slash invocation is user-verified. Browser, preview, and supporting tool behavior can vary by setup. |
| Codex | `$ak:motion-video ...` | The Skill uses native Codex discovery. Hook projection is partial; provider, render, and FFmpeg requirements are unchanged. |

### Pick or mix a style

Every look is a style profile: a compact motion-design grammar for palette,
typography, layout, material, texture, camera, transitions, pacing, beat
response, UI choreography, diagrams, character motion, captions, and sound.
`references/styles/index.yaml` lists about 70 of them. The printed and
hand-made family includes an editorial collage explainer (Vox-like), mid-century
retro, punk zine, constructivist poster, WPA screenprint, groovy seventies, ink
woodblock, newsprint editorial, and art deco luxury. Brand names such as
Apple, Nike, Linear, or NVIDIA are aliases that lead to a canonical motion
language (for example, Apple-like leads to `cinematic-product-launch`); the
output never uses a brand's logos, fonts, or assets.

- **One style:** name it, for example "comic style" for `comic-multiverse`.
- **Mixed:** keep one base and borrow individual dimensions for particular
  scenes. `python scripts/resolve-style.py resolve data/style.json --script data/script.json`
  resolves the mix into a per-scene table that goes into the plan.

Three styles also ship a detailed reference: `comic-multiverse`,
`glass-keynote`, and `cinematic-product-launch`. The working projects behind
their demos are not bundled; every new video starts from the Skill's
composition skeleton plus the resolved style.

### Compare styles with a bake-off

When the brief names no style, or two candidates tie, the Skill picks 3–4
profiles that fit the topic's era, culture, and tone, builds the same hook
scene once per candidate from the skeleton, snapshots its busiest moment, and
shows the stills side by side. You pick by eye, and the pick becomes the base
style. A bake-off generates no audio, so it runs before any paid audio
generation.

### Direct the story and shots

The style decides how the video looks; the story and shot plan decides what it
says, in which order, and how each scene is framed.

- **Narrative arc.** The Skill picks one of 14 arcs for the topic, such as
  `hook_payoff` (the default for a single idea), `pas`, `how_it_works`,
  `timeline`, `myth_buster`, `listicle`, or `release_tour` for launch and
  changelog videos (hook, headline number, features with the strongest first
  and last, proof, outro).
- **Hook and ending.** The first spoken line and the first on-screen words
  carry the promise within 3 seconds, using one named hook pattern such as
  `surprising_stat` or `direct_question`. The ending is `hard_cut`,
  `quick_cta`, `loop_close`, or `signature_outro` (the default for releases).
- **Scene count.** Scenes are sized to the duration: about 6–8 scenes for
  30 seconds, 10–12 for 60 seconds, and 14–16 for about two and a half minutes.
- **Shots.** Each scene gets a shot size (`EST_WIDE`, `WIDE`, `MEDIUM`,
  `CLOSE`, `DETAIL`) and one camera move on its camera group: `static`,
  `push_in`, `pull_out`, `pan`, `tilt`, `parallax`, or `element`. Bolder moves
  such as `orbit`, `roll`, `whip`, and `dolly_zoom` are used only when the base
  style's camera dimension allows them.
- **Anti-monotony.** Adjacent scenes never repeat a camera move, and `static`
  is kept for the payoff. Something visibly changes every 3–5 seconds: a scene
  longer than about 7 seconds gets an internal cut-in from a wide framing to a
  close on a spoken keyword, so no framing holds past about 8 seconds.

The arc, hook, ending, and per-scene shot are recorded as optional `arc`,
`hook`, `ending`, and `shot` fields in `data/script.json`.

Before any paid audio is generated, the Skill shows you the beat map once: the
arc, the hook, and one line per scene. It skips this review when you supplied
the final script.

### Add generated still art

Some printed or hand-made looks, such as collage, woodblock, or poster styles,
cannot be drawn convincingly with CSS and SVG. For those, the Skill can
generate still illustration layers through the `multix` CLI, using one image
route for the whole video:

| Condition | Image route |
| --- | --- |
| `codex` CLI on `PATH` | multix's OpenAI image route through the local Codex CLI |
| otherwise, or if the Codex route fails | multix's Gemini image route |

Pieces are generated on a flat chroma background, cut out locally with FFmpeg,
baked to transparent PNG layers under `assets/images/`, and animated with GSAP.
The Skill reviews every image before animating it and records how each asset
was made in `data/art.json` so a re-roll reproduces the look. No video model is
involved; AI text-to-video or image-to-video footage still belongs to
[`ak:video`](./video).

- **Text stays live HTML.** Headlines are never baked into generated images,
  so type stays crisp and every fact stays editable.
- **Supplied photos are placed, not redrawn.** A photo of a person or product
  you supply is placed as a photographic cut-out. The face, product shape, and
  label stay as in the photo, and the Skill re-checks them in the generated
  stills before cutting them out.
- **UI, data, terminal, and diagram scenes stay live HTML.** Generated art is
  for printed or hand-made imagery only.

## Follow the production stages

1. **Pin the outcome.** Topic, sources, base style and any layers, duration,
   voice language, ending, and signature are recorded. If the style is still
   open, the bake-off settles it here.
2. **Scaffold the project.** The Skill creates `assets/videos/<slug>/` and a
   plan under `plans/`, then copies the template scripts, example data files,
   `hyperframes.json`, and the composition skeleton. A finished edition is never
   overwritten; a restyle is a new project directory.
3. **Write facts and script.** Facts come only from primary sources, with a short
   evidence report before scripting. The Skill picks the arc, hook, and
   ending, sizes the scenes, and plans each scene's shot. `data/script.json`
   holds the scenes and lines. You review the beat map once here, unless you
   supplied the final script.
4. **Generate and align audio.** multix produces the voice-over, SFX, and music;
   ElevenLabs forced alignment maps spoken words to timing.
5. **Fit the beat and arrange music.** `fit-beat-grid.py` reports BPM and a
   per-bar table; bars are spliced so drops land where the script needs them,
   and `verify-arrangement.py` checks the splice.
6. **Build the timeline and mix.** `build-timeline` renders the ducked,
   loudness-normalized mix; `measure-mix-balance.py` reports music under speech.
7. **Build and validate the composition.** When the style needs printed or
   hand-made imagery, or you supplied a photo to place, the generated art
   layers are baked first. `index.html` follows the composition contract, the
   resolved style, and each scene's planned shot, then passes
   `hyperframes lint`, `check`, and scene `snapshot` review, plus the
   director's pass on every scene.
8. **Render, remux, and encode.** HyperFrames renders the video, the measured mix
   is remuxed back in, and an optional social encode produces a smaller file.
9. **Add a square or vertical edition, when requested.** A re-layout edition
   (`scripts/reframe.py --ratio 1x1|9x16`) reuses the master's voice-over,
   music, and mix and adds only ratio-specific CSS and its own render; a quick
   ffmpeg fit or crop is a faster, lower-fidelity alternative for previews.

## Keep generation, spend, and publishing gated

<Callout type="warn" title="A video brief does not authorize provider spend">
  Voice, SFX, music, and alignment requests go through multix to Gemini and
  ElevenLabs. Regenerating scenes or music creates more provider requests.
  Generated still art is an extra, optional image-generation request on top of
  the audio requests, and a bake-off may use one generated backdrop per
  candidate style. Approve each generation stage under your provider, quota,
  and billing policy.
</Callout>

| Work category | Normal boundary |
| --- | --- |
| Local mutations | The workflow creates a project directory, plan, data files, generated audio, snapshots, and renders. It does not overwrite a finished edition. |
| Provider and network effects | multix sends script text and audio to Gemini and ElevenLabs. With generated art, it also sends image prompts and any photo you supplied for placement to OpenAI through the local Codex CLI, or to Gemini. `npx --yes hyperframes@0.8.77` can fetch the pinned package. |
| Processes | `hyperframes preview` runs as a tracked background process that is reused for this project and stopped when the session ends. |
| Publishing and account effects | The Skill produces the file and leaves posting to you. It does not upload, schedule, or post to social accounts. |

Brand aliases select a motion language only. Confirm rights for fonts, images,
music, voices, and any product visuals, and verify every on-screen claim against
the collected sources.

## Review outputs and evidence

A project typically contains:

```text
assets/videos/<slug>/index.html
assets/videos/<slug>/hyperframes.json
assets/videos/<slug>/data/script.json
assets/videos/<slug>/assets/audio/{vo,sfx,music}/
assets/videos/<slug>/renders/<slug>.mp4
assets/videos/<slug>/renders/<slug>-social.mp4
```

With generated art, the project also holds the baked layers under
`assets/images/` and their provenance in `data/art.json`.

The Skill reports measured numbers, not impressions. It is done only when:

- `hyperframes lint` and `check` pass and snapshots of every scene were
  reviewed, and every scene passed the director's pass.
- `verify-arrangement.py` reports every segment within ±2 ms, and
  `measure-mix-balance.py` shows the music about 4–6 dB under speech.
- `ffprobe` reports the expected frame size (1920×1080 for the master, or the
  requested 1:1/9:16 edition's own size), 30 fps, the expected duration, and
  AAC 48 kHz stereo; loudness is about −14 LUFS with peak at or below −1 dBFS,
  and blackdetect finds nothing outside fades.
- `build-timeline` printed no clip, overlap, or missing-word warnings.
- Each requested 1:1 or 9:16 edition passes the same lint, check, ffprobe,
  loudness, and blackdetect checks as the master.
- On-screen facts trace to the sources collected at the start.
- No two adjacent scenes share a camera move, and no framing holds past about
  8 seconds.

## Troubleshoot safely

| Symptom | Safe next step |
| --- | --- |
| Runtime cannot find `ak:motion-video` | Confirm target and scope, start a fresh session, then follow [Runtime cannot find a Skill or Agent](../../../troubleshooting/runtime-cannot-find-skill-or-agent). |
| `multix` is missing or a provider call fails | Stop before generation, keep the script and data files, and fix multix or its own credential config. Do not copy keys into the project. |
| Alignment warns that aligned words and script tokens differ | Fix the scene's `en` or `say` text, or regenerate that scene's voice-over, then realign. |
| `verify-arrangement.py` reports drift | Re-fit the beat grid, adjust the arrangement, and re-run the arrangement and timeline steps. |
| `lint` or `check` fails | Fix the composition against the composition contract before rendering. |
| The Codex image route fails (not signed in or a driver error) | The Skill falls back to the Gemini image route for the whole video and notes it in the plan. Sign in to `codex` before the next video if you want the OpenAI route. |
| Only visuals changed | Re-run `lint`, `check`, `render`, and the remux; audio does not need regeneration. |
| A 1:1 or 9:16 edition still shows 1920/1080-sized layout | Read `reframe.py`'s warnings for the affected lines, add the missing `data-ratio` override, and regenerate the edition; never hand-edit the generated edition directory. |
