---
title: Chain Skills with workflow guides
description: Follow complete, reviewable workflows across the Engineer and Marketing Kits instead of choosing isolated Skills.
---

A Skill owns one focused outcome. A workflow connects several Skills while
preserving decisions, evidence, artifacts, review gates, and unresolved risks
between calls. Use these guides when the result requires more than one Skill or
must move between the Engineer and Marketing Kits.

## Choose a workflow

| Goal | Guide | Typical chain |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deliver a feature, repair a bug, or ship a verified UI change | [Software delivery](./software-delivery) | ak:brainstorm → ak:plan → ak:cook → ak:test → ak:code-review |
| Plan a launch, improve a funnel, or run an evidence-backed marketing cycle | [Marketing growth](./marketing-growth) | ak:marketing-research → ak:marketing-planning → execution Skill → ak:analytics |
| Coordinate product implementation and launch work across both Kits | [Cross-Kit product launch](./cross-kit-product-launch) | Marketing discovery → Engineer delivery → Marketing launch and measurement |

Each guide includes prerequisites, prompts, handoff artifacts, stop conditions,
and verification. The chain is a recommended sequence, not permission to skip
review or perform an external action.

## Carry context between Skills

For every transition, pass the smallest useful handoff:

- the accepted outcome and non-goals;
- decisions already made and evidence supporting them;
- files, plans, briefs, reports, or test artifacts produced;
- failed checks, unresolved questions, and the next stop condition;
- the authority still required for publishing, deploying, spending, merging,
  or modifying an external system.

Use Engineer [ak:handoff](../engineer/skills/handoff) or Marketing
[ak:handoff](../marketing/skills/handoff) when the workflow crosses a session,
owner, runtime, or Kit.

## Invoke the chain

Claude Code and Cursor use names such as /ak:plan; Codex uses $ak:plan for supported
Skills. Invoke one Skill at a time, review its output, and then pass the approved
result to the next call. Do not paste a whole chain as one command.

Stop when an input is missing, evidence contradicts the plan, a check fails, or
the next step needs authority you do not have. Resume from that failed
prerequisite rather than silently continuing.
