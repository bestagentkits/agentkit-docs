Use this when progress stalls: requirements keep shifting, two approaches both
feel wrong, or you want an honest second opinion before more code.

<Callout type="info">
  Advisory skills interview one question at a time — answer specifically. Full
  flag lists: [Engineer kit cheatsheet](../kits/engineer).
</Callout>

## When to use

- You are about to commit to a design and want it grilled.
- The ticket is a pile of symptoms without a goal.
- Implementation is thrashing and you need a smaller next move.

## Workflow

<Steps>
<Step>

### Reframe the problem

```text
/ak:advise Should we build our own job queue or use the managed one?
```

`/ak:advise` scouts when useful, interviews for missing constraints, then
returns what to do, what to avoid, trade-offs, and a short checklist. Bring a
URL (issue, spec) when you have one.

</Step>
<Step>

### Widen or narrow options

```text
/ak:brainstorm Job queue decision for webhook delivery under 30s
```

When multiple viable paths remain, brainstorm up to a few options with real
trade-offs and a recommended smallest path. Do not invent components the
constraints do not require.

</Step>
<Step>

### Unstick structured reasoning

```text
/ak:problem-solving We keep reintroducing N+1 queries after each feature
```

Use problem-solving when the pattern is recursive (same class of failure) or
the constraint set feels self-contradictory. Pair with `/ak:debug` if the stall
is a concrete defect, not a strategy question.

</Step>
</Steps>

## Sample prompts

```text
/ak:advise Grill this plan: plans/…/plan.md — what would you cut?
/ak:brainstorm Offline-first notes app — MVP vs sync-later
/ak:problem-solving Complexity spiral in the billing module
```

## Pitfalls

- **Interview as delay** — answer the advisory questions; do not stall for a
  perfect brief.
- **Advice without a decision** — end with a chosen path or an explicit defer.
- **Using advise as implement** — after the decision, go to
  [the core loop](./core-loop) or [debug and fix](./debug-and-fix).
