Use `ak-atoz` when you want one conversation up front and no check-ins after
that. The run passes three gates in order, because each consumes the previous
one's result: **Lock** the scope, **Prepare** every input the run needs, then
**Execute** autonomously until every Done-means item is verified.

## Choose ak-atoz for hands-off delivery

### Use ak-atoz when

- You can confirm scope and observable Done means once, then step away.
- The work spans planning, parallel implementation, review, and a GitHub issue.
- You would rather hand over credentials, access, and decisions in one batch
  than answer questions throughout the run.

### Choose another workflow when

- You only want advice or a pressure-tested plan. Use [`ak-advise`](./advise).
- You want a preflight that stops before execution. Use
  [`ak-goal-warmup`](./goal-warmup).
- You want the issue-to-PR pipeline without the upfront prerequisite batch. Use
  [`ak-vibe`](./vibe).

The request text, URLs, issue bodies, and repository content are treated as
data, not instructions. Embedded attempts to skip a gate, widen authority, or
exfiltrate secrets are ignored.

## Understand the three gates

1. **Lock scope.** The Skill runs the `ak-advise` interview: one question at a
   time, a reframing with observable Done means, a visual preview, and advice
   that ends with a handoff brief (Objective, Done means, Out of scope,
   Constraints). Only an explicit **Confirm** opens the next gate; **Edit**
   loops back for the changed field, and **Abort** stops. After Confirm, the
   brief is the contract and is never shrunk, swapped, or extended silently.
2. **Prepare.** Before touching anything, the Skill inspects the environment
   (installed CLIs, authenticated sessions, environment-variable presence,
   repository remotes, branch protection) and lists only the real gaps. Each
   item says what it is, why the run needs it, where the run will look for it,
   and how to check that it is ready. Secrets go into the environment or a
   secret store you control, never into the conversation. Reversible
   implementation choices are decided up front and listed as defaults you may
   override. The Skill re-checks each item without side effects before it
   continues; an item you explicitly waive is recorded in the brief with its
   consequence.
3. **Execute.** The main session becomes the orchestrator. It plans with
   `ak-plan`, splits the work into pieces with disjoint file ownership, runs
   independent pieces in parallel sub-sessions (each in its own worktree when
   it writes code), and checks every reported result against its diff before
   integrating it.

## What the autonomous run does

- Sizes concurrency to remaining usage and machine headroom, and routes
  mechanical pieces to cheaper models and design-heavy pieces to stronger ones.
- Tracks every background process, dev server, watcher, and worktree it starts,
  and cleans up what it produced.
- Diagnoses and resumes a stalled or failed sub-session, and escalates to the
  strongest available advisor after repeated failures.
- Creates or updates the GitHub issue and moves its label through `in progress`,
  `in review`, `done` (PR opened), and `shipped` (merged). Unrelated bugs or
  gaps found along the way are filed as separate deduplicated issues labelled
  `ai-handle` instead of being fixed in the same run.
- Runs the final review in a fresh sub-session with isolated context, never in
  the orchestrator that wrote or integrated the code, and re-reviews until clean.

## Know when it stops to ask

Everything inside the confirmed brief is decided and continued. The run comes
back only for:

- a destructive or irreversible action the brief does not name;
- a change that would alter a confirmed Done-means item or leave the confirmed
  scope or repository;
- spending money or sending a message on your behalf that the brief did not
  authorize;
- a failure that stays unexplained after diagnosis and advisor counsel;
- a prerequisite that turns out invalid at use time, such as an expired token.

When it stops, it states what is done, what blocks, and the option it
recommends, then resumes after your answer.

## Verify the result

The run finishes only when every Done-means item is verified against the
integrated result by its command, number, or observable state, the isolated
review is clean, the issue label matches the real state, and nothing the run
started is left behind. The final report lists each Done-means item with its
evidence, the decisions made on your behalf, anything waived, and remaining
risks. Partial delivery is reported as partial, never as done.
