Use `ak:marketing-ideas` to explore a focused set of marketing options for a
SaaS or software product. The Skill asks for product, audience, stage, goal,
budget, team, and prior attempts, then recommends three to five catalog ideas
with a starting path.

## Choose ideation before commitment

### Use ak:marketing-ideas when

- You need several plausible ways to reach, educate, acquire, activate, retain,
  or re-engage a defined audience.
- You want options filtered by stage, resources, budget, timeline, product, and
  current evidence.
- You want to compare content, search, product-led, community, partnership,
  event, paid, email, platform, localization, or unconventional approaches.

### Choose another workflow when

- You need evidence that an audience, problem, or channel exists. Use
  [ak:marketing-research](./marketing-research).
- You need a sourced competitive comparison. Use
  [ak:competitor](./competitor).
- You have selected an option and need phases, owners, measures, and approval
  gates. Use [ak:marketing-planning](./marketing-planning).

## Prepare the decision context

Provide the product and business model, target customer, market, current stage,
main goal, available team and skills, budget range, timeframe, existing
channels, previous results, brand constraints, measurement capability, and
actions that are out of bounds.

Attach evidence where possible. A tactic becomes more relevant because it fits
the stated context, not because it appears in the catalog. If the context is
missing, the Skill should ask rather than recommend a generic growth list.

## Understand the recommendation stages

1. **Clarify context.** Confirm product, audience, stage, goal, resources,
   budget, timeline, prior work, constraints, and decision owner.
2. **Filter the catalog.** Select three to five ideas whose prerequisites and
   likely time horizon fit the context; avoid returning all 140 entries.
3. **Explain fit.** For each idea, connect the recommendation to supplied
   evidence and label unsupported reasoning as an assumption.
4. **Define a safe start.** Give the first two or three planning or validation
   steps, resources required, dependencies, and a reversible test where
   possible.
5. **Compare options.** Show trade-offs, risks, measurement needs, and the
   decision that would favor one option over another.
6. **Hand off without execution.** Return the recommendations for review, then
   use research or planning separately before any external action.

The canonical output fields are idea name, one-line description, why it fits,
how to start, expected outcome, and resources needed. Treat “expected outcome”
as a hypothesis and success definition, not a forecast.

## Screen risky ideas before planning

<Callout type="warn" title="The catalog is inspiration, not evidence or permission">
  The source calls its approaches “proven” but supplies no citations, effect
  sizes, success rates, or applicability tests. Validate every idea against your
  market, users, policies, platform terms, and current law.
</Callout>

Some catalog entries can create material effects: paid ads, retargeting and
pixel sharing, audience uploads, engagement pods, giveaways, referral or
affiliate incentives, contract buyouts, lifetime deals, localized pricing,
acquisitions, sponsorships, outreach, public comparisons, controversial
positioning, data publishing, or account creation. Do not execute or recommend
deceptive engagement, undisclosed endorsements, unauthorized data sharing,
spam, discrimination, dark patterns, false scarcity, or unsupported claims.

Ideation does not authorize network calls, provider use, spend, collection of
personal data, customer contact, publication, pricing changes, contracts, or
production changes. Each effect needs a separately reviewed plan and approval.

## Verify the options and evidence

A useful recommendation set should include:

- The supplied facts and assumptions used to filter the catalog.
- Three to five options with fit, non-fit, prerequisites, cost category, effort,
  time horizon, owner, and dependencies.
- A falsifiable hypothesis, leading and guardrail measures, evidence needed,
  and a reversible validation step for each option.
- Ethical, privacy, legal, brand, platform, accessibility, financial, and
  operational risks where relevant.
- Rejected ideas and the reason they do not fit now.
- The next decision and approval required before research, planning, or action.

## Troubleshoot and know the limits

| Symptom | Safe next step |
| --- | --- |
| Recommendations are generic | Add product, audience, stage, goal, resource, prior-result, and non-goal context, then ask for fewer options. |
| An idea depends on an audience claim | Run research or customer validation and keep the idea labeled as a hypothesis. |
| A platform tactic may be outdated | Verify the platform name, feature, policy, access, and current availability before planning. |
| The option implies spend or data sharing | Stop at a plan; obtain finance, privacy, legal, security, and account-owner approval as applicable. |
| “Expected outcome” reads like a guarantee | Rewrite it as a testable hypothesis with baseline, measure, timeframe, and stop condition. |

The catalog is oriented to SaaS and software and includes platform references
that can age. It defines no scoring formula, citation set, benchmark, provider,
price, or performance guarantee, and it names no canonical output file. Continue
with [ak:marketing-research](./marketing-research) to validate assumptions or
[ak:marketing-planning](./marketing-planning) to create an approved plan.
