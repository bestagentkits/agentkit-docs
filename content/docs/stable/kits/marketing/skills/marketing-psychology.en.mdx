Use `ak:marketing-psychology` to examine a specific audience decision through
behavioral and strategic models, then turn the diagnosis into transparent,
testable marketing guidance. The catalog spans foundational thinking, buyer
behavior, persuasion, pricing, delivery, and growth models; it is a decision
aid, not proof of how any individual thinks.

## Choose a behavior question with an ethical boundary

### Use ak:marketing-psychology when

- You know the desired behavior, journey stage, audience evidence, and current barrier.
- You want relevant models explained and translated into reviewable hypotheses.
- You can preserve truth, meaningful choice, accessibility, and informed consent.

### Choose another workflow when

- You need clinical, mental-health, diagnostic, or individual profiling advice.
- You want to exploit vulnerability, addiction, fear, financial distress,
  children, sensitive traits, or impaired decision-making.
- You need final copy, page implementation, or an experiment build. Use the
  appropriate workflow after the hypothesis and safeguards are approved.

## Prepare context and evidence

Provide the exact behavior, audience and exclusions, journey stage, observed
friction, research or analytics evidence, current experience, brand rules,
legal or platform constraints, and success plus harm measures. Separate
observed behavior from inferred motivation. Use aggregate research where
possible and do not attach psychological labels to identifiable people.

## Understand the stages

1. Confirm the behavior, evidence, audience, context, and authority boundary.
2. Diagnose the barrier before selecting models; check simple operational causes first.
3. Choose the smallest relevant model set and explain mechanism, uncertainty,
   alternative explanations, and likely second-order effects.
4. Convert each model into an ethical hypothesis with truthful content,
   reversible choice, accessibility, and harm guardrails.
5. Define a bounded test with success, guardrail, segment, duration, and stop measures.
6. Present guidance for human review; implementation and publication remain separate.

## Keep persuasion ethical and reviewable

<Callout type="warn" title="Influence must preserve agency">
  Do not use fake scarcity, fabricated authority or social proof, misleading
  anchors, hidden fees, obstructed cancellation, preselected paid options,
  coerced consent, disguised advertising, or fear-based pressure. Defaults and
  framing should be visible, truthful, easy to reverse, and lawful.
</Callout>

Treat claims such as the “Rule of 7,” 80/20 distributions, or fixed loss
aversion ratios as models to test, not universal laws. Review pricing,
subscription, advertising, privacy, and consumer-protection requirements for
the audience and jurisdiction before use.

## Verify outputs and evidence

The Skill defines no fixed output path. A useful result includes the observed
barrier, evidence and gaps, selected models, mechanism, ethical application,
rejected manipulative alternatives, hypothesis, variant guidance, success and
harm measures, approval gates, and limitations. Ensure every testimonial,
count, scarcity statement, price comparison, credential, and outcome claim is
current and supportable.

## Troubleshoot and know the limits

| Symptom | Safe next step |
| --- | --- |
| Too many models fit | Return to the observed constraint and choose the smallest testable set. |
| Motivation is inferred as fact | Label it a hypothesis and gather qualitative or behavioral evidence. |
| A tactic reduces meaningful choice | Reject or redesign it with clear disclosure, opt-out, and reversal. |
| Results improve but harm rises | Stop the test and review complaints, vulnerable segments, refunds, and long-term trust. |
| Runtime cannot find the Skill | Follow [Runtime cannot find a Skill or Agent](../../../troubleshooting/runtime-cannot-find-skill-or-agent). |

The Skill does not guarantee persuasion, conversion, retention, revenue, or a
correct account of customer psychology. See [Marketing Kit](../../marketing).
